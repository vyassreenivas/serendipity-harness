53. Interview 53

4 stories. A former corporate operations leader left corporate life after burnout and launched an operations agency with no plan, drawing on her skills. COVID-19 wiped out her client roster overnight; after a stint at a marketing agency she declined a corporate job, went all in, bootstrapped and grew quickly through networking and referrals. The one event told as unexpected is the COVID loss of all clients; the rest is a story of planned effort, persistence and network-driven growth.

  • S1 leaving corporate to launch: After burnout ended a long corporate operations career, she jumped into her own business without a strategy, relying on her skills and refining her offers over years, including a failed virtual assistant business first.
  • S2 the COVID client loss: The pandemic blindsided her, losing every client within a day and flattening the business, after which she took work with a marketing agency.
  • S3 going all in: When the agency job ended she turned down a corporate offer on gut feeling against advice, bootstrapped with small retainers, and grew from her first 10k month to 50k months.
  • S4 networking and referrals: Clients came from going to conferences and socializing, and later mainly from client referrals and word of mouth.

A: Oh, I have learned from so many mistakes and failures. The same goes for your business. Try to think big picture and eliminate yourself as the bottleneck. Q: Advice for other entrepreneurs who want to get started or are just starting out?
[21]A
A: My go-to saying is, “Imperfect action is better than perfect inaction.” There is a point where you need to STOP consuming and start acting. Entrepreneurs often get stuck in a cycle of consuming multiple mentorships, courses, community groups, etc. I’m not saying those things are bad, but at some point, you need to DO THE THING! You will fail. Not to be a negative Nancy, but that is just life. You will make mistakes, lose money, learn, pivot, and make money again. That is entrepreneurship. It is something new, not corporate, not set in stone, and oppressive or limiting. Business is what you make it, so make it a place where your team and clients can buy in and be proud of the ethos together. Look forward to your next failure, embrace feeling uncomfortable, and don’t forget to take a moment to celebrate while you move on to the next big thing.
