6 stories. A product designer left home to push herself into new situations; as she travelled her freelance income surprisingly rose and creators asked how, so she started an online school. When her own workload filled up she began passing clients to designers in her network, realised matching was a business, and [COMPANY] took off with organic leads. Later threads: shock at development quotes pushed her to no-code, an async sales approach clients loved despite warnings, email marketing that drew praise but no conversions, an over-hasty scaling that produced a loss month, and an ad test that brought more demand than they could handle. No luck language is used.

  • S1 [PRODUCT] school: While travelling and freelancing her income unexpectedly rose; creators asked how she earned so much, and she started an online school teaching designers to find clients.

Q: Take us through the process of building the first version of your product.
[7]A1.3S31.4S31.5S31.6S32.6S33.1S34.4S34.5S35.4S36.1S36.6S37.2S37.3S37.4S3
A: As a designer, jumping into the product design part was super fun. I worked with my designer to create all the front-end designs. Yet, when it came time to develop, I was shocked by the price it would cost to build. Agencies were quoting me about $400K and over a year of development. So I decided to go no-code and built on Bubble. I felt like the designer side of the platform did not need much validation as it’s simply a place where designers can find jobs, upload their portfolios and manage those jobs. Yet, the client side of the experience was much more opinionated. At [COMPANY], we have a fully asynchronous sales cycle. I was told this wouldn’t work since our average contracts are over $6,000 per month. Yet, our clients loved the no-pressure approach to finding a designer. We built the first client experience in WordPress and using Loom for sales, we created an MVP sales experience. Since then, we’ve iterated and improved the client experience significantly, but I’m so happy I went with ultralow code for the client experience as it changed quite a bit from inception. Solve a problem that already exists. I spent the first few years of my entrepreneurial career trying to convince people they had a problem. When I found a need, and just solved it better - things got much easier.

Q: Describe the process of launching the business.
[9]A4.3S27.2S28.4S2
A: We never really launched, which is funny to stay. We incorporated in 2021, bought insurance for our business, launched our website, and in 2022 we already had our first customers. The costs were relatively low to start, and I used my consulting money to finance. Since the start of the business, I’ve invested about $40K of my own money into product development and some early hires. One thing we did that was special was we hired a brand strategist right away. We have a pretty comprehensive brand book that talks a lot about our unique value proposition, how we communicate as a company, and our values. The second lesson I learned was, a good product doesn’t take much marketing. I had heard from many gurus that great products fill needs and existing desires. In the past, I created what I thought people wanted and would try and convince them to buy it. With [COMPANY], I looked at what people have been asking for, and what founders needed and just created a solution.

Q: Since launch, what has worked to attract and retain customers?
[11]A1.3S41.4S41.5S41.6S42.6S44.4S46.6S4
A: Since day one, we’ve focused on SEO. We don’t have a lot of traffic but a high website conversion rate. I think being present where the founders are is a better solution for us. We’ll be expanding to paid media next year.
