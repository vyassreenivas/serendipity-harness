65. Interview 65

3 stories. A CS graduate builds a geocoding API in grad school because he needs one for his own projects and none exists; demand for it grows and a business plan follows. In 2016 he rewrites it as a global API, made worthwhile by newly available open geodata, at near-zero cost and building on his earlier [EARLIER COMPANY]. The API sits unnoticed for months, gets a journalist as a first free user and paying clients from late 2017, and takes off in 2018 for reasons he says he cannot explain and did not cause.

  • S1 the geocoding API for himself: In grad school, before Google Maps, he built a geocoding API he needed for his own projects; he never validated it, and as demand grew a business plan grew to pay for features others requested.
  • S2 going global with open data: In 2016, on a working vacation, he rewrites his North American API into a global one, because enough open geodata had become available; costs are near zero and it builds on [EARLIER COMPANY].
  • S3 getting noticed in 2018: The live API goes unnoticed for about six months; a journalist becomes the first user and he adapts the API to that use; paying clients arrive from December 2017 and in 2018 it takes off, which he cannot explain and says was not his doing.

Q: Take us through the process of designing, prototyping, and manufacturing your first product.
[7]A1.5S21.6S22.9S24.2S24.3S25.4S26.1S27.2S2
A: Designing a geoparsing/geocoding software is a complex tech process. It combines NLP techniques with Computational Geometry algorithms and fast in-memory databases. It took about a month or so to build the first geocode api back in 2004. It only worked for Canada, and was limited to forward geocoding. Later on it grew to include the US. The process usually goes as follows - write scripts to clean up and import data from different sources. Validate and test. Tweak the software to deal with more edge cases (without affecting anything else). Estimate various performance and accuracy metrics and finetune further towards the ever closer goal of 100% coverage and accuracy. There is always an inverse tradeoff between performance and accuracy. Now the API can guess with a high degree of probability geocodes from (possibly misspelled/incomplete) locations mentioned in text. The north american version of [EARLIER COMPANY] (USA & Canada) has taken 14 years to build, and I’m still tweaking it every now and then. Building an API that works globally though, was a different kind of beast. In 2016 I started working on a complete re-write that is now [COMPANY], while on vacation in Spain. (I like taking working vacations) There was enough open geodata available globally due to the success of the OpenStreetMap project and various government open data initiatives, to make the effort worthwhile. The new rewrite of the software uses reinforcement learning techniques to geoparse and disambiguate locations from text of any length, which are then geocoded by matching them against existing geocoded datasets based entirely on open data. In the beginning the startup costs were close to zero (I bought the [COMPANY] domain name during a domain sale in 2016 for $1) For servers I used a free cloud instance on AWS and I also released the software on AWS Marketplace for those requiring better performance (which is directly related to the specification of the provisioned server) About two years have passed and the costs of running the business have somewhat increased alongside revenues. I make money from AWS Marketplace. The software is rented by the hour. It costs between under $1/hr to about $6/hr, depending on the number of CPU’s on the hardware. AWS handles all customer relations, so that part of the business is on autopilot. I also make money by selling API access directly That pricing page was only recently put up, for a while the API was free and most of the business model was based on AWS. Although the free version is now more restricted via throttling, I have picked a pricing model for API access that is very cheap compared to the closest competitors. While some have gone the other way and actually increased pricing, for eg Google Maps Platform, I’ve kept the pricing simple and low. [COMPANY] offers unlimited plans for a flat monthly fee of about 200EUR as well as pay-as-you go options.
