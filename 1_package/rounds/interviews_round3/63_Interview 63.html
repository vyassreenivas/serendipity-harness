63. Interview 63

4 stories. An IT administrator finds deploying applications for a company painfully slow, researches the market and builds a cheaper, simpler web tool. The MVP took about a year on free-tier tools, sped up by advances in AI; it launched free through a Reddit post, and growth later came from Microsoft MVP creators. A Microsoft announcement of something similar made them delay and nearly abandon the launch, costing time.

  • S1 the deployment project idea: While working on a project deploying applications for a company he found it very time-consuming, researched solutions, and saw room for a cheaper, simpler web-based tool.
  • S2 building the MVP: With little coding experience they build the MVP over a year on low-cost and free-tier tools, a small advance from the co-founder, and development speeds up with advances in AI.
  • S3 the Reddit launch: They release the product free with a casual Reddit post, get mostly positive feedback and criticism, and the first purchase request arrives while he is at a wedding; later growth comes via Microsoft MVP creators.
  • S4 Microsoft's similar product: Microsoft announced a similar product, so they delayed the launch and almost gave up, losing time in the market.

Q: How did you launch [COMPANY] and get initial traction?
[11]A7.1S37.2S3
A: We launched via a well-crafted Reddit post to share the news of our MVP. We didn't want it to sound overly professional and money focused and more about sharing something we had built as a side hustle. This approach helps when you offer the product completely free, which we did as Reddit users don't take kindly to sneak advertising. The reddit users were mostly positive feedback and constructive criticism, with a few trolls in there. In regard to lessons, I would have put more time into design and tested with a smaller group before unleashing to the wild as I was putting out fires like no tomorrow. As for our first dollar, I was actually at a wedding when we got the first request come through to purchase an annual license. I was so excited I generated the invoice from my phone while at the wedding.

Q: What was the growth strategy for [COMPANY] and how did you scale?
[13]A7.1S3
A: While we initially grew [COMPANY] using a reddit post. We have since expanded into working with Microsoft MVPs who run YouTube channels or blog posts and have been able to show demos of the product to their subscribers. Since working with these professionals, we have seen a massive growth in our customer base. We also run a private Facebook group with about 1K members where we share new features and updates we are working on. We also have a budget for running ad campaigns but its been hard to track the metrics of how much this is leading to paid subscriptions. My recommendation to entrepreneurs is to find a niche in the market of something you are good at. Build a working MVP and then share your work on Reddit, offering the service for free. Be prepared for harsh and sometimes downright mean feedback but it's the best way to get your product out there for free.
