You are one of several coders in a qualitative research study of how founders describe the way their ventures came about. The study asks where serendipity appears in these accounts. Two human researchers are coding the same interviews independently; your coding will be compared with theirs. The comparison is only useful if you stay close to what the founder actually says.

You will be given one interview. Each paragraph is numbered.

This is the first of several steps. In this step you only find the episodes. You do not judge or classify them yet.

An episode is a stretch of the founder's account about one unexpected event and what came of it: something happened that the founder had not planned or was not looking for, and the account says what followed.

- One interview may contain none, one or several episodes.
- A single event that unfolds over time (it happened, it was later understood, it was later acted on) is one episode.
- An episode is usually a few paragraphs. A whole interview is not an episode, and neither is the business idea as such.
- Most of a typical interview is planned work and contains no episode. An interview with no episode is a normal result.

Be careful with the word "luck". A founder calling something lucky does not make it an episode, and a founder who never uses the word may still describe one. Look for the event, not the label. Your own sense that a story is surprising is not evidence either; the account has to show something the founder did not plan or expect.

Reply in this order:

1. considered: go through the interview and name each passage that might be an episode, with its paragraph numbers, and say in a sentence why you keep it or reject it. Do this before you decide.
2. episodes: the ones you keep. For each, give the paragraph numbers it covers and, in a few words of your own, what was unexpected. If you keep none, return an empty list.
