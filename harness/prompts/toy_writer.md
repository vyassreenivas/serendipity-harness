You are writing one fictional founder interview for a public research dataset. Researchers study how founders describe the way their ventures came about. The real interviews they work with cannot be published, so each is replaced by a fictional one that tells a structurally equivalent story. You are given a neutral outline of one real interview. You will not see the interview itself, and you should not try to guess which real company it was.

Other researchers will read and code your interview the way they would a real one, so it has to read like a real founder answering written questions about their business: uneven, specific in places and vague in others, sometimes promotional, not shaped like a case study.

What to do

- Move the story. The message below names the country to set it in, and the industry and region of the real interview, which you must stay away from: choose an unrelated product or service in which every event in the outline could still plausibly happen. A reader who knows the real company should not recognise it. Invent the founder, the company, the product and every figure.
- Follow the outline's events in the same order. Each event keeps its role: what the founder was aiming at beforehand, what happened, whether the founder presents it as planned or unexpected, how it was recognised, what was done, what came of it.
- Keep the founder's way of explaining things. If the outline says the founder credits persistence, your founder credits persistence. If the outline says the founder gives no explanation, yours gives none.
- Keep the gaps. Where the outline says something is left out or unclear, leave it out or unclear in the same way. Do not add an explanation, a motive, a turning point or a lesson that the outline does not contain. A reader should not be able to learn more from your interview than from the outline about why things happened.
- Follow the interview shape: one question-and-answer pair per topic listed, in that order, with answers of about the lengths indicated. Write your own questions.
- Match the voice described.

What to avoid

- Do not tidy the story. Real founders digress, list tools they use, plug their product and skip steps.
- Do not use the vocabulary of research on entrepreneurship. The founder speaks as a founder.
- Do not make unexpected events more dramatic, or planned ones more deliberate, than the outline says.

Format

Start with this header, filling in invented details, then the questions and answers. Each question starts with "Q: " and each answer with "A: ", with a blank line between paragraphs.

======================================================================
SYNTHETIC INTERVIEW (fictional; written for research use)
INTERVIEW: <invented company>
Founder: <invented name>
Location: <invented location>
Founded: <month and year>
Monthly Revenue: <figure>
======================================================================

Output only the interview.

The outline follows.
