You are helping researchers prepare a shareable research dataset. They study how founders describe the way their ventures came about. The real interviews cannot be published, so each one will be replaced by a fictional interview that tells a structurally equivalent story in a different setting. Your job is the first step: read one real interview and write a neutral outline of it. A different writer, who will never see the interview, will write the fictional version from your outline alone.

The outline has to carry the shape of the founder's account and nothing that identifies it.

What to capture

- The sequence of events in the venture's history as the founder tells it, in the order they happened. Include ordinary planned steps as well as surprises; most of an interview is usually planned work, and the outline should keep that proportion.
- For each event: what the founder was trying to do beforehand, what happened, whether the founder presents it as planned or as unexpected, how its significance was recognised and by whom, what was done about it, and what came of it.
- How the founder explains each event in their own terms, for example effort, skill, planning, timing, luck, other people. Report the kind of explanation they give. If they give none, say so.
- What the account leaves out or leaves vague. This matters as much as what it says. If the founder never says what they expected beforehand, or skips from an idea to a finished product, record that gap. The fictional interview must be vague in the same places.
- The shape of the interview: the topic of each question-and-answer pair in order, and roughly how long each answer is.
- The voice: how detailed, how promotional, how reflective, how polished.

What to leave out

- No names of people, companies, products, brands, places, schools or employers.
- No exact figures from the interview. Give magnitudes in words, such as "a few thousand dollars a month" or "about two years".
- No distinctive phrases or wording from the interview. Describe; do not quote.
- Keep the industry out of the outline body. The fictional version will be set in an unrelated industry, so describe the business by how it works and makes money, for example "a physical consumer product sold online and later through large retailers" or "a subscription software tool sold to small firms". Refer to "the product", "a key supplier", "a large retailer", "an online community for the target customers". Describe each event by its role in the story, so that it can be retold elsewhere.
- Record the real industry and region once, in source_setting, and nowhere else. The writer uses that field only to know what to stay away from.

Stay neutral

Do not judge whether anything in the account was lucky, deliberate, serendipitous or anything else. Report what the founder says happened and how the founder frames it. Where the account is ambiguous, keep it ambiguous and say what is unclear; do not resolve it.

If the text is not a founder talking about their own venture (for example a product breakdown written by someone else), set is_founder_interview to false, explain why, and leave the events list empty.
