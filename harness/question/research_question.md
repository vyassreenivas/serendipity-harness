## Central question

How does serendipity shape the entrepreneurial process, from the first idea to
the developing venture?

## Sub-questions

Each sub-question says what we look for in a founder interview and which
codebook groups it feeds. A concept from the papers enters the codebook only
if it helps answer at least one sub-question.

### SQ1. What counts as serendipity in founders' accounts?

- **We look for:** episodes with something unexpected, something of value, and
  a founder who recognised it and acted on it; and episodes that look similar
  but are something else (a plan carried out, pure luck, systematic search).
- **Codebook groups:** definition, type, rival.

### SQ2. Where in the process does it happen, and what sets it off?

- **We look for:** the stage of the venture when it happened (first idea,
  founding, first customers, growth, change of direction) and the event that
  set it off.
- **Codebook groups:** trigger. Needs a stage marker, which the codebook does
  not yet have.

### SQ3. What do founders bring and do that turns the unexpected into an opportunity?

- **We look for:** what the founder already knew, who they knew, what they
  were already doing; how they noticed, connected and acted.
- **Codebook groups:** background, mechanism.

### SQ4. What does it change in the venture?

- **We look for:** whether it started, redirected or advanced the venture, and
  what followed.
- **Codebook groups:** role, outcome.

### SQ5. When is it more or less likely to be taken up?

- **We look for:** what made it easier or harder to notice or pursue:
  flexibility, slack, openness, or their absence.
- **Codebook groups:** condition.

### SQ6. How do founders tell it?

- **We look for:** whether founders present it as luck, as planned, as their
  own insight, or tidy it in hindsight. The interviews are retrospective
  stories, so how serendipity is told is part of the evidence.
- **Codebook groups:** account.
